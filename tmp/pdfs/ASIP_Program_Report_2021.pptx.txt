The past several months have been trying for humanity the world over. From the first report of the 
Covid-19 virus to the global border and economic shutdowns that followed, the youthful African 
continent and indeed the world, has definitely never faced a more trying period in this era. But as 
is typical of the African spirit, innovation across the continent never faltered. Instead, it soared and 
became the propellant needed to drive forward, conversations around the regulatory and policy 
changes much needed across the continent – AfCFTA – and in key markets – Nigerian startup bill 
and the Senegalese Startup Ministry. 
We’re seeing innovation happen in more depth and enjoying discovering and providing massive 
support to innovative efforts in the remotest parts of Africa. With our years of deep experience 
innovating in and for this market, we have an extensive understanding of local context and 
cultural nuances and are best suited to jointly develop and scale disruptive solutions. And we’re 
doing this irrespective of Language barriers – as proven by our establishment of an Innovation 
Campus in Dakar, Senegal in Partnership with the DER HUB and the Startup Ministry of Senegal 
– and the many challenges that still abound in this market. 
The core mission of Startupbootcamp AfriTech is to scout, skill and scale these pan-African 
companies so they can compete on a global stage. Accelerators help founders move along the 
validation curve much faster by providing opportunities to work closely with some of the most 
innovative corporations on the continent. We compress 18-24 months of growth into a 3 month 
programme and help generate rapid commercial traction. 
SBC AfriTech is a proudly African solution with global brand-backing - and the first ever instance 
where multiple corporations on the continent have embraced the concept of open innovation and 
collaboration to solve critical business challenges. Working together, we have made exciting 
progress. 
We welcome more leading African corporations to join us in launching new programmes that 
empower the next generation of African Innovators. 
Resilient Innovation in Covid Times

INNOLEAPS
Founded in 2013, Innoleaps helps global companies like Unilever, Vodafone, Liberty Global, 
Philips, Rabobank, ING Bank, JDE, FrieslandCampina, Ahold Delhaize, Heineken, Vivat and 
many other Fortune 500 companies with:
•Developing an innovation portfolio 
•Business model innovation
•Thinking and Acting like startups
•Corporate Startup Engagement
•Shifting from a managerial to an intrapreneurial culture
•Enabling open innovation
Innovation is  creating new growth from non-incremental innovation. Most companies are great 
in making existing products better, but are less qualiﬁed in developing, testing and scaling new 
business models.
How do companies create new growth models more systematically? How do you manage the 
startup to scale-up process in a corporate environment? Accelerators, hackathons and other 
innovation vehicles are one side of the coin but to really make an impact you should also be 
organized to accelerate growth.
The question becomes; “where is the money to be made in the future?” We help you determine 
growth opportunity areas to focus innovation activities and connect innovation strategy with the 
corporate strategy. The dominant pattern is that established companies allocate their resources 
by focusing on executing or optimizing their core business. We spot disruptive opportunities, by 
deep diving in the periphery of your market. We scout relevant startups, detect unsolved 
customer needs and create alignment on board level around your innovation strategy and 
portfolio. 
We install growth boards acting as an internal investor towards their own startups. We will teach 
you how to apply the principles of validated learning and metered funding. We will help you 
build a small venture capital unit that oversees the innovation portfolio, allocate necessary 
resources and make sure the growth path of corporate startups is managed eﬀectively.
Based on our extensive experience and helping more than 175 corporate startups we will:
•Bring innovations faster to market - we accelerate corporate startup teams from new idea to 
repeatable success in the market in just 6-9 months
•Make innovations more successful - According to Harvard Business Review, the global 
average for corporate Product Launches is 14%. Companies using the Innoleaps methodology 
have seen an average success rate of 70%

===== PAGE 21 =====
The secret sauce behind the Startupbootcamp model is Corporate-Startup Collaboration. Our main focus is 
to form meaningful partnerships and synergies between emerging and established businesses. As the ﬁrst 
multi-corporate-backed accelerator to launch in Africa in 2017, our partners have been able to ﬁnd new 
lines of revenue, provide solutions for their customers, gain innovation insights, improve internal 
processes, and leapfrog competition by innovating faster. 
The COVID-19 pandemic came with insurmountable challenges, chief among them being the disruption of 
all industry verticals. Covid accelerated the disruption across the entire value chain and has impacted every 
aspect including logistics, retail, agriculture, ﬁnance, credit, health, and education, to name a few. 
In the face of this tectonic shift, corporates struggle to adapt quickly. Large corporates have long 
decision-making processes and many stakeholders to consider. This creates fertile ground for startups to 
step in with radically new solutions and quick innovation models. The pandemic has shown companies the 
importance of adaptive strategies that spearhead innovation internally. 
MAIN GOALS FOR CSC 
Based on our research, there are two main goals that companies pursue through working with startups 
• ●  Running PoC’s and integrating validated solutions 
●  Accelerating the company’s growth 
• Factors such as learning about new business models and enhancing the company’s internal 
innovation scored lower in this section. The results of the survey also revealed that for the 
participants, getting insights in startup-, technology- and market trends was not a reason for 
establishing CSC. This can be related to the fact that almost all of the companies surveyed are 
already searching /planning to search for the right startups and scale-ups to collaborate with 
internally

===== PAGE 23 =====
According to reports by Disrupt Africa, 397 African startups in 2020 received over $700 million in 
investments. Compared to 2019, there was a slow growth - most likely a response to the disruption 
caused by the Covid - 19 Pandemic. Despite the interference, there was an increase of over 10% in 
investments in African startups. Major sectors that had the most investment in 2020 were Fintechs, 
E-commerce/retail tech, E-health and Logistics and the investment maintained by the big four: 
Nigeria, Egypt, South Africa and Kenya amidst the highest investment in Nigeria. 
Startups in Africa have raised over $2.9 billion - with about 200 at $1m based on a report by Maxine 
Bayer in October 2021. With the highest funding rounds occurring in August and September to an 
enormous amount of over $1 million, Nigeria is still leading the investment rounds with 44% 
followed by South Africa and Egypt. The fintech sector leads the investment with 57% followed by 
energy and education/jobs. 
These investments show the confidence investors have in Africa regarding the FINTECH sector 
despite the stifling regulations set by the Apex bank in Nigeria and other regulatory bodies. African 
entrepreneurs constantly show their commitment to solving problems of the continent thus attracting 
international funding.

===== PAGE 30 =====
In February 2020 Africa had its first coronavirus case in Egypt and by March the several other 
African countries confirmed cases which led to the declaration of national disasters and a 
continent-wide lockdown which still persists. 
The COVID-19 pandemic became the much-needed catalyst for the mass adoption of digital 
technologies, especially for communication and the accelerator of the concept of the future of 
work which had been a theory for decades. 
We began to live our lives through lockdowns; with hard lockdowns on one end and soft 
lockdowns on the other. 
Hard Lockdowns
On hard lockdowns residents living in hot spots were not allowed to step out of their houses 
except for essential work and to get essential commodities, including
edible items and medicines. 
Technologies that became popular during these lockdowns were ones that enhanced
communication with friends, family and colleagues, provided more digital content and those that 
made goods and services more accessible.
