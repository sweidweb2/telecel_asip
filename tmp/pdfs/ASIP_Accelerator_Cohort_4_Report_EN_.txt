FOREWORD
On the 26th of March, 2021, we received the first set of
applications for the ASIP accelerator cohort 1 following its
launch a few weeks before. 4 cohorts later, we have come to
the end of this chapter. As I reflect on the journey that has led
us to this moment, I am filled with gratitude and excitement.
The ASIP Accelerator Program has been a beacon of
innovation, collaboration, and growth for all involved. From
the initial spark of an idea to the culmination of months of
hard work, dedication, and perseverance, this report stands as
a testament to the remarkable achievements of all the startups,
corporate partners, and various stakeholders we’ve engaged
and interacted with on the journey the past 4 cohorts.
Throughout this program, we have witnessed the
transformative power of creativity, resilience, and community.
We have seen founders come together in constellations to
build solutions to leading challenges faced by members of the
larger community within the market clusters they operate in.
Entrepreneurs from diverse backgrounds and industries have
also come together, driven by a shared passion to bring their
visions to life. They have embraced challenges as
opportunities, turning obstacles into stepping stones toward
success.
In these pages, you will find the stories of bold ventures,
breakthrough innovations, and meaningful connections
forged during our time together. Each project represents not
only the culmination of countless hours of effort but also the
promise of a brighter future. From disruptive technologies to
sustainable solutions, these ventures are poised to make a
lasting impact on their industries and communities.
I am immensely proud of what our participants have
accomplished and inspired by the passion and dedication they
have demonstrated every step of the way. As we celebrate their
achievements, let us also recognize the invaluable support and
guidance provided by mentors, advisors, and partners who
have been instrumental in this journey.
It's a wrap!

The General Delegation for Rapid Entrepreneurship of
Women and Youth DER/FJ is an institution attached to the
General Secretariat of the Presidency of the Republic of
Senegal. The goal is to facilitate the implementation of
strategies that foster employment of young people and
women, the mobilization of resources to increase access to
capital, and to foster entrepreneurship projects that help
increase economic well-being. Its actions have been guided by
a common vision: to catalyze economic development,
encourage innovation, and create tangible opportunities for
women and young entrepreneurs in Senegal.
Since its launch in 2018, the DER/FJ has carved an
exceptional path in the Senegalese entrepreneurial landscape.
The projects supported by the team have not only created jobs
but also helped build a resilient and dynamic entrepreneurial
community. Billions of CFA francs have been mobilized to
support thousands of projects bringing hope and prosperity.
These results, well beyond simple statistics, embody the
dreams and ambitions of women and young entrepreneurs.

Dan Berlin, a dynamic entrepreneur, investor, philanthropist,
and accomplished endurance athlete, defies expectations as a
visionary individual with visual impairment. With a strong
commitment to creating opportunities globally, he draws on
over 12 years of experience in major multinational
corporations.
Co-founding Rodelle, a world-renowned vanilla extract
company, marked a significant chapter in Dan's
entrepreneurial journey. The company achieved a milestone
acquisition by Archer-Daniels Midland Company in 2018, with
Dan successfully navigating his role as CEO until the age of 48.
Beyond his business achievements, Dan is the Co-founder and
President of Team See Possibilities, a non-profit organization
dedicated to empowering young individuals with vision loss.
Through scholarships and mentorships, the organization
supports high-achieving college students, contributing to their
thriving futures. Simultaneously, as the chairman of Success
Beyond Sight, another nonprofit, Dan works towards
eliminating barriers that hinder equal opportunities for
professionals with vision impairment.
Dan extends his influence by direct financial investment in the
ASIP Holding fund. Notably, he has been the most active
mentor in the program for the past three years, providing
invaluable guidance and introductions to startup founders.
His involvement extends to scaling new SBC AfriTech
programs, contributing to the establishment of follow-on
funds, and nurturing the growth of fund managers. Dan
Berlin exemplifies resilience, vision, and a commitment to
fostering positive change in both the entrepreneurial and
philanthropic realms.

The competitive advantage behind the SBC model is
Corporate-Startup Collaboration (CSC). Our main focus is to
thrive from meaningful partnerships and synergies between
emerging and established businesses.
As the first multi-corporate-backed accelerator to launch in
Africa in 2017, our partners have been able to find new lines
of revenue, provide solutions for their customers, gain
innovation insight, improve internal processes, and leapfrog
competition by innovating faster.
The Covid-19 pandemic came with insurmountable
challenges, chief among them being the disruption of all
industry verticals. Covid accelerated the disruption across the
entire value chain and has impacted every aspect including
logistics, retail, agriculture, finance, credit, health, and
education, to name a few.
In the face of this paradigm shift, Corporates struggle to adapt
quickly. Large corporates have long decision-making processes
and many stakeholders to consider. This creates fertile ground
for startups to step in with radical new solutions and quick
innovation models. The Pandemic has shown companies the
importance of adaptive strategies that spearhead innovation
internally.

With these collaborations, SBC AfriTech seeks to create a
startup ecosystem where businesses can share knowledge
about challenges they face, critical resources, and skills that
improve their innovation and competitiveness. ASIP's
knowledge-sharing and skill-exchange programs are essential
tools for promoting entrepreneurship and innovation in
Senegal, resulting in increased productivity, the creation of
significant partnerships, networking opportunities, and
improved capacity building.
We have also pioneered an open innovation approach that is
more distributed, more participative, and more decentralized,
based on the observed fact that today's useful knowledge is
widely distributed and that no company, no matter how
capable or how large, could innovate effectively on its own.
We have organized open innovation workshops and corporate-
startup matching events built around constellation paths,
enabling companies, organizations, start-ups, and players in
specific focus areas to share knowledge and information and
collaborate on sustainable solution paths guided by our
specialists. 
And that's how we've seen the emergence of partnerships
where companies join forces with start-ups or players in the
informal sector to find new solutions to difficult problems and
design a pilot to validate all assumptions around these new
solutions.

INVESTMENT
TRAJECTORY
Global investments in venture capital reached US$144 billion
in the first half of 2023, with double-digit negative growth in
both Q1 and Q2, culminating in an overall 50% decline from
the US$293 billion invested in 2022 H1. Familiar
macroeconomic constraints, including stubbornly high
interest rates, European geopolitical instability, and concerns
related to the stability of the global banking system, are
partially attributable to this funding winter.
However, record losses by industry titans, including the
US$5.5 billion investment loss in Q4’22 for SoftBank’s Vision
Funds; a 33% markdown of the portfolio value for Tiger
Global’s venture capital funds; and the dissolution of Sequoia
Capital (arguably the industry’s biggest global brand) into
three independent regional entities in June 2023, have also
rocked the boat and magnified turbulence in the VC market
this year. After reaching a peak of close to US$187 billion in
Q4’21, global VC investment has fallen in each subsequent
quarter to stand at US$65 billion in Q2 2023.
