Foreword
Accelerating in a Downturn!
The year 2023 for African startups and 
the entire ecosystem began (quite 
unsurprisingly) with a major downturn 
in VC funding. According to data from 
Disrupt Africa, there’s been a 57.2% 
drop in funding based on recorded 
funding rounds anounced between 
January 1st - 31st March 2023, and the 
corresponding time period for 2022.

Thus we launched the 3rd Cohort of the 
Startupbootcamp Afritech ASIP 
Accelerator, which began on the 20th 
of February, 2023, at a not so great time 
for investors in our network and 
available funding opportunities for 
startups on the program.

Inspite of this and many more 
structural challenges that comes with 
the work we do, we were able to select 
from the far corners of the African 
continent, some of the most promising 
startups, and work with them to 
achieve some really remarkable 
results, with major economic & 
ecosystem impacts for Senegal our 
operating country, and our founders 
who individually are in the process of 
raising a collective $4.5m, and 
significant progress for the SBC 
Afritech team towards securing pilot 
funding for the constellation Project for 
startups in our future Programs.
Startupbootcamp AfriTech scouts, 
skills, and scales disruptive pan - 
African startups, allowing them to 
compete in global markets. We help 
founders validate their solutions by 
fostering collaboration with Africa’s 
most forward-thinking corporations. 
SBC AfriTech compresses 18 - 24 
months of growth into a 3-month 
programme and helps generate vast 
commercial traction. And we’re the 
leading Accelerator and Venture/
ecosystem builder by virtue of Alumni 
valuation (over $850m collectively on 
over 60 startups) and impact in local 
operational markets.

Partnering to 
Empower Innovation
SBC Afritech's partnership with 
ENRICH in Africa is a significant 
milestone in the startup accelerator's 
efforts to promote entrepreneurship 
and innovation in Africa. The 
initiative, funded under the European 
Union's Horizon 2020 research and 
innovation program, aims to bring 
together stakeholders from Africa and 
Europe and support the growth and 
development of the African innovation 
ecosystem.

Through its partnership with ENRICH 
in Africa, SBC Afritech has been able to 
attract additional funding to support 
its efforts in identifying and nurturing 
high-potential startups in Africa. 
Furthermore, the sponsorship of five 
startups as part of the program has 
been a significant boost to SBC 
Afritech's operations, providing them 
with access to additional resources, 
networks and expertise.
SBC Afritech's startups have benefitted 
from the networking events, capacity 
building, and other support provided by 
the ENRICH In Africa project, enabling 
the development and expansion of 
various services and products. 
Moreover, SBC Afritech has also been 
able to leverage ENRICH in Africa's 
expertise and extensive network to 
gain access to a broader pool of 
opportunities for its startups, thus 
increasing the likelihood of those 
startups breaking into new markets 
and achieving success.

SBC Afritech's partnership with 
ENRICH in Africa reflects its 
commitment to work with global 
partners to promote innovation and 
entrepreneurship across borders. It is a 
testament to the organization's focus 
on creating a robust and well-
supported entrepreneurial ecosystem 
in Senegal, which ultimately leads to 
the economic growth and development 
of the country.

and endurance athlete who happens to

Beyond Sight, a nonprofit organization

impairment to advance professionally, 
by eliminating barriers towards equal 
opportunity.

investment into the ASIP Holding fund,

introductions for the startup founders.

AfriTech programs and building

Competitive Advantage
Corporate-Startup Collaboration
The competitive advantage behind the
SBC model is Corporate-Startup 
Collaboration (CSC). Our main focus is
to thrive from meaningful partnerships 
and synergies between emerging and 
established businesses.

As the first multi-corporate-backed 
accelerator to launch in Africa in 2017, 
our partners have been able to find 
new lines of revenue, provide solutions 
for their customers, gain innovation 
insight, improve internal processes, 
and leapfrog competition by 
innovating faster.

The Covid-19 pandemic came with 
insurmountable challenges, chief 
among them being the disruption of all
industry verticals. Covid accelerated 
the disruption across the entire value 
chain and has impacted every aspect 
including logistics, retail, agriculture, 
finance, credit, health, and education, 
to name a few.

In the face of this paradigm shift, 
corporates struggle to adapt quickly. 
Large corporates have long decision-
making processes and many 
stakeholders to consider. This creates 
fertile ground for startups to step in 
with radical new solutions and quick 
innovation models. The Pandemic has
shown companies the importance of
adaptive strategies that spearhead
innovation internally.

Notable Impact in the Senegalese Ecosystem
Job Creation
SBC AfriTech has played a crucial role 
in generating employment 
opportunities for Senegalese citizens.  
One of the most significant ways in 
which ASIP achieves this goal is by 
encouraging and facilitating startups 
to incorporate in Senegal to expand the 
reach of their product or service. 

By setting up shop in Senegal, these 
startups can penetrate the local 
market, create jobs, and contribute to 
the growth of the local economy. SBC 
AfriTech's efforts have catalyzed a 
surge of entrepreneurial activity in 
Senegal, with more businesses looking 
to establish themselves in the country. 

Overall, SBC AfriTech's support of 
startups incorporating in Senegal has 
been a significant contribution to the 
socio-economic development of the 
country.
Digital Innovation
SBC AfriTech's focus on digital 
innovation has had a significant 
impact on the socio-economic fabric of 
Senegal. As a startup accelerator, ASIP 
is committed to identifying, nurturing, 
and supporting startups in the digital 
space. 

This approach has led to the 
emergence of new and innovative 
solutions to address the various 
challenges facing communities in 
Senegal. 

Furthermore, ASIP provides a platform 
for startups to showcase their digital 
innovations, which could potentially 
attract investment and create new 
business opportunities. 

SBC AfriTech's emphasis on digital 
innovation has also created a pool of 
skilled professionals in Senegal, which 
further contributes to the country's 
development by promoting knowledge-
sharing and entrepreneurship. 

Overall, SBC AfriTech's focus on digital 
innovation has been a significant 
catalyst for economic growth and 
development in Senegal.

African Trends &
Future Trajectory

===== PAGE 41 =====
The year 2022 proved to be a momentous one 
for the African tech industry, with the sector 
surpassing both the US$1 billion and US$2 
billion marks for the first time, signaling an 
unprecedented growth trajectory. Despite a 
global dip in investments, particularly in 
riskier asset classes such as venture capital, 
the African tech space showed no signs of 
slowing down in 2022. A record-breaking 633 
African tech startups raised a combined 
US$3,333,071,000 over the course of the year, 
surpassing the US$3 billion mark for the first 
time. While the growth rate was slightly 
slower than the previous year, the number of 
funded startups increased by 12.2%, reaching 
564, and the total secured funding jumped 
55.1% to US$2,148,517,500.

The number of startups securing investment 
has been increasing year on year. In 2015, only 
125 startups secured investment; this figure 
grew to 146 the following year and continued 
to rise to 159 in 2017, 210 in 2018, 311 in 2019, 
and 568 in 2021. Though growth slowed in 
2020, it picked up again in 2021, reaching a 
record high. The number of African startups 
securing investment each year has increased 
by 406% since 2015.

The amount of total funding going to African 
tech startups also continues to increase, 
although the growth rate decreased 
significantly in 2022. When records began in 
2015, African startups raised a combined 
US$185,785,500. This total fell to 
US$129,113,200 in 2016 but then began to rise. 
In 2017, total funds raised increased by 51% to 
US$195,060,845; they then soared a further 
71.5% to reach US$334,520,500 in 2018, grew 
46.7% to US$491,623,400 in 2019, and went up 
another 42.7% to reach US$701,460,565 in 2020.
In 2021, the total funding for African tech 
startups rose an extraordinary 206.3% to 
US$2,148,517,500. Although the growth rate 
slowed to 55.1% in 2022, African tech startups 
still received an impressive US$3,333,071,000 
in funding. This increase of over 50% is a 
notable achievement for the sector, which is 
experiencing a decline in global venture 
capital investments.

Total annual funding for African tech startups 
has increased by over 1,000% since 2015. The 
size of the average deal increased by 38.2% to 
US$5,265,515 from US$3,285,181 in 2021, almost 
double the US$1,766,903 figure recorded in 
2020. Although average deal sizes have 
fluctuated over the past few years, they now 
seem to be generally trending upwards. The 
2020 figure represented an 11.8% increase on 
the US$1,580,783 figure recorded in 2019.

In 2022, 276 startups raised US$1 million or 
more, representing 43.6% of the total. This was 
up from 206 (36.5%) in 2021, which was up 
from 110 (27.7%) in 2020 and 81 (26%) in 2019.
The record for the largest round ever raised by 
an African tech startup was again broken in 
2022. Nigerian fintech startup Flutterwave 
topped its own 2021 record with a US$250 
million raise in February. Other notable 
amounts were raised by Nigerian mobility 
fintech company Moove (US$181.8 million 
across five investments), Egyptian fintech 
platform MNT -Halan (US$150 million), 
Algerian super app Yassir (US$150 million), 
Kenyan retail-tech startup Wasoko (US$125 
million), Tunisian AI startup InstaDeep 
(US$100 million), South African e-commerce 
company Clickatell (US$91 million), and 
Kenyan P A YG solar company M-KOP A (US$75 
million).

===== PAGE 42 =====
Despite the increase in investors, growth in 
the sector slowed down in 2022. At least 987 
disclosed investors contributed to African tech 
startups, a 28% increase from the 771 recorded 
in 2021. This is the largest number of 
individual disclosed investors in any year on 
record. However, as growth slows, the 2021 
figure represented an over 100% increase from 
the 370 disclosed investors in 2020. The 
numbers for 2019 and 2018 were 261 and 155, 
respectively. (Disrupt Africa, 2022).

These investors come in various shapes and 
sizes. The most active are early-stage funds 
such as Launch Africa Ventures, LoftyInc 
Capital Management, and Future Africa. Both 
local and international accelerators are also 
very active, including Y Combinator, 
Techstars, Flat6Labs, and Startup Wise Guys.
At later stages, there are many international 
and local VC firms active in the market, with 
major global investors such as Tiger Global, 
Sequoia Capital, and SoftBank increasingly in 
the mix. Successful African founders, such as 
Paystack's Shola Akinlade and Flutterwave's 
Olugbenga Agboola, are also active investors.
Disrupt Africa, 2022

In 2022 the main stages of startups invested 
in, were Seed and Pre-Seed which together 
made up 70% of all investments. Compared to 
2020, later stages such as Series-A, Series-B, 
and Series-C have shown slight increases 
which supports the increase in VC and 
corporate engagement. The type of funding 
was mainly based on equity investments, with 
only 4.8% reporting debt financing. With these 
stages, the majority of funded startups were 
founded during the pandemic (Disrupt Africa, 
2023).

The sectors and industries of interest were 
still dominated by FinTech. Although 
expected in 2021, investors have shown the 
highest recorded interest in FinTech startups 
nearly making up 50% of all funded startups.
This drive evolved mainly from Nigeria and 
Egypt due to the previously mentioned record 
holders in funding amount. Still, other 
industries have also shown to double their 
funding in value with E-commerce and 
RetailTech experiencing a 271.5% increase, 
Transport of 102.4%, Logistics of 134%, and 
EdTech experiencing an astonishing 515.3% 
increase from the year before (Disrupt Africa, 
2022).

Overall, every startup industry has grown 
substantially which sheds light on the 
promising future for African entrepreneurs. 
Compared to our ASIP application round, it is 
apparent that most applicants are agriculture 
related, although most “AgriTech” solution are 
not innovative at all. FinTech remains the 
most promising sector, also for SBC AfriTech.

===== PAGE 44 =====
One can conclude that African Startups have a 
very promising future, but there are still key 
issues which need to be addressed. Still, only 
21.5% of startups had female representation in 
the founding teams. Countries with the 
highest share of female representation were 
Kenya with 32.2%, Nigeria with 24.2%, Ghana 
with 22.2%, South Africa with 14.6%, closely 
followed by Egypt, Morocco, and Tunisia with 
13.9%, 13.3% and 13.3% respectively. Female 
empowerment is gaining popularity but at a 
slow pace (Disrupt Africa, 2022).

Just 37.8% of startups participated in some 
kind of incubation or acceleration program of 
which many have female representation as 
requirement. Pushing these requirements and 
fostering female founded companies in 
particular, could result in visible changes in 
following years. The overall social impact still 
remains positive.

From the reported startups, 17,915 Jobs were 
created in total, which are 32 new jobs per 
startup. The most impactful cluster 
developments were reported in Nigeria, Egypt, 
Kenya, and South Africa, of which 88.7% were 
African founded startups (Disrupt Africa, 
2022).

Another trend we will see in the future is that 
with increased funding and corporate-VC 
engagement, capital deepening will allow for 
more readily available resources. This 
development has been criticised with the 
question, if such access to capital would 
actually be beneficial for startups? 
Although capital deepening will always be a 
factor that fosters economic growth and will 
even effect the startups on an operational 
level , it is not a determinant of economic 
development. 

Startups will be expected to show higher 
growth because of more access
to capital. This however, could be a crucial 
mistake which may foster unproductive or 
even destructive entrepreneurship. Although 
financial inclusion has been one of the most
detrimental issues for African entrepreneurs, 
other institutional factors still pose barriers. 
These de jure factors will not change as quick 
as corporates and VC might wish, and could 
cause perverse incentives for unsustainable 
decision making focused on growth. Thus, it 
is, and always be important to have the right 
medium for funding which could be an 
accelerator or incubator.

Fine dosage of knowledge and capital, paired  
with industry specific mentoring and 
consulting still remains the most effective 
and sustainable approach to foster 
entrepreneurial and economic development. 
Prone to repeat history and debt intolerance, it 
is not the level of capital stock but the quality 
which determines the stability of many 
African countries. With this in mind, SBC 
AfriTech is aimed at fostering 
entrepreneurship, not just to achieve 
exponential growth but to sustain continuous 
development. The latter is the difficult part, 
but it also the most rewarding.
