03 
Voice, Language & Copy Discipline 
Short, confident, human and commercially credible, with no corporate filler 
USE AVOID 
Build, solve, test, launch, grow, scale, commercialise, prove, 
move, back, open, apply. 
Empower, enable, leverage, facilitate, foster, synergise or 
“support” without saying how. 
Founders, products, markets, customers, pilots, outcomes, 
sectors, growth. 
Institutional abstractions, CSR language, self-congratulation 
or vague ecosystem claims. 
Short headlines, one clear idea, proof underneath. Headline + sub-headline + paragraph + caption stacks that 
repeat the same thought. 
African-rooted confidence with international ambition. Deficit framing or language that presents African founders as 
needing rescue. 
Investor-ready facts and verified portfolio signals. Unverified “deal flow”, “investment opportunity”, valuations 
or return claims. 
“Connecting Energies” only as a deliberate brand line. Repeating connect/connected/connecting as generic 
verbs across modules. 
 
DENSITY RULE One screen, one job. Hero support copy: maximum 35 words. Standard homepage modules: generally 
20-45 words. Cards: one idea in 10-25 words. Move detail into profiles, accordions or deeper pages. 
 
House style 
• British English in running copy: programme, organisation, commercialise, prioritise, behaviour. 
• Sentence case for headings. Use title case only where it is part of a formal programme or brand name. 
• Use numerals for metrics, dates and cohorts. Use commas in thousands and consistent + notation for rounded 
figures. 
• Do not use exclamation marks in core corporate copy. 
• Do not use em dashes. Use commas, colons, parentheses or full stops. 
• Never use “Africa” redundantly in every headline when ASIP already establishes the geography. 
• Do not describe ASIP as “the largest”, “leading” or “number one” unless a current independent source 
supports the claim.

06 
About ASIP 
A concise origin story, clear purpose and a build-log style journey 
HERO 
Started in 2019. Still building. 
ASIP launched in Barcelona to give ambitious African technology founders stronger access to expertise, 
markets, investors and commercial opportunity. The model has evolved, but the focus remains the same: 
help good companies move faster. 
Our story 
PUBLIC COPY 
Built around founders who know the problem first-hand. 
Africa does not lack ideas. The harder gap is often access: to the right expertise, market insight, 
technology, capital pathways and commercial relationships at the right moment. ASIP was created to 
narrow that gap. Since 2019, the programme has worked with founders across sectors and markets, helping 
teams sharpen what they are building, test growth assumptions and move towards stronger businesses. 
Mission 
PUBLIC COPY 
Help ambitious founders build stronger companies. 
ASIP identifies technology ventures solving meaningful problems in African markets and strengthens the 
company behind the idea: product, strategy, market access, investor readiness and commercial 
opportunity. 
Vision 
PUBLIC COPY 
African technology companies built for local relevance and global 
scale. 
We want more category-defining companies to be built from the continent, grow across borders and 
compete on the strength of their products, teams and market insight. 
Belief 
PUBLIC COPY 
Local insight is an advantage. 
The strongest products often begin with people who understand the problem first-hand. ASIP exists to help 
founders turn that insight into businesses that can grow beyond one customer, one city or one market.

What is ASIP? ASIP is Telecel Group’s Africa Startup Initiative Program, launched in 2019 to help ambitious

Which sectors does ASIP 
support?

What does Telecel Group 
contribute?

Can investors access the
